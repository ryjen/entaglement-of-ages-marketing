# Public series visual continuity

This document defines public-safe visual continuity for *Entanglement of Ages* across covers, site imagery, approved illustrations, and any future published sequential-art material.

It is a **presentation specification**, not a canon or authoring surface. It must not reproduce private plot architecture, symbolic rationale, prompts, scene notes, rejected concepts, or unpublished character information.

## Four-book visual identity

The books should feel like parts of one dramatic series while remaining unmistakably different eras.

| Book | Public visual impression | Prefer | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Age of Embers* | exposed, glacial, migratory, physically immediate | cold scale, dangerous sky, water, routes, small human groups, carried resources | brown cave-museum styling, heroic caveman portraiture, generic shaman imagery |
| *The Fatherless* | institutional, political, human, socially compressed | civic architecture, thresholds, crowds, cold material realism, people embedded in hierarchy | sepia antiquity, devotional imagery, Jesus-like hero staging, Biblical-poster composition |
| *Neurion* | networked, plural, civic, distributed | many active centres, lived infrastructure, humans and synthetic persons sharing space, partial failure | one glowing AI centre, generic cyberpunk, Matrix code, robot-saviour imagery |
| *The Age of Forms* | inherited, selective, comparative, beautiful but uneasy | unevenly surviving advanced systems, social selection, visibility, contrast between representation and lived personhood | generic post-apocalypse, floating-score dystopia, social-media parody, a single demographic presented as the objective ideal |

Book IV currently reuses shared site styling while its dedicated public theme is still being developed. New Book IV imagery should follow this direction without assuming an unapproved final palette or technology design.

## Shared cinematic grammar

Across all four books:

- people should exist inside environments, institutions, crowds, infrastructure, and relationships rather than appearing as isolated icons;
- material scale and access should communicate power before decorative symbolism does;
- environmental and architectural detail should feel physically plausible for the depicted era;
- compositions may echo one another across books when the visible similarity works without revealing hidden story architecture;
- no image should be necessary to understand public page copy;
- public artwork must remain understandable without private production notes.

## Public-safe continuity cues

The following visual relationships can recur publicly without explaining their private narrative significance:

- people beneath large horizons or environmental scale;
- routes, roads, channels, and movement through space;
- thresholds and differences in access;
- water and reflection where materially appropriate;
- one person contrasted with a crowd or many independent actors;
- records, interfaces, or representations shown as partial views of a larger reality;
- ordinary human relationships inside larger systems.

Do not publish a private motif map explaining what these cues mean across the series. Public materials should stand on their visible dramatic function alone.

## Character continuity

Approved narrative or illustrated editions may establish a stable visual design for recurring characters. Public marketing should not assume that every exploratory or generated concept is definitive casting.

Before publishing recognizable character art:

- confirm the design is approved for public use;
- check age, clothing, injury/body-state, and setting continuity against the approved source;
- avoid phenotype as shorthand for morality, intelligence, virtue, corruption, or worth;
- avoid familiar religious or genre casting that changes the apparent story;
- ensure the image reveals no embargoed relationship, identity, survival state, or later-story development.

## Sequential-art derivatives

If panels or pages from a graphic/illustrated edition are published on the site, treat them as governed excerpts rather than ordinary decorative images.

They require:

- explicit excerpt/publication approval;
- spoiler-tier classification;
- rights and provenance review;
- metadata review;
- accessible alt text appropriate to what is actually visible;
- responsive derivatives where needed;
- manifest coverage before entering deployable `src/`.

A panel approved inside a narrative edition is not automatically approved as a standalone marketing asset because context can change both spoiler risk and interpretation.

## Difficult material

Public promotional imagery should be more conservative than in-context narrative art around sexual coercion, incest, childbirth, slavery, torture, execution, humiliation, or other severe violence.

Do not use context-dependent suffering primarily as a click-driving hero image. If difficult material is publicly excerpted, the surrounding copy and placement must preserve the intended human context rather than turning it into spectacle.

## Cross-era restraint

Do not force visual continuity by repeating literal symbols.

Avoid:

- zodiac glyphs as explanatory graphics;
- supernatural beams connecting ages;
- repeated halos or chosen-person lighting;
- seven fixed icons for the Seven Sins;
- a single logo-like object inserted into every era;
- visual effects that imply an author-confirmed metaphysical explanation.

The series should feel connected through composition, material relationships, and recurring human pressures rather than a puzzle-box symbol system.

## Private-to-public handoff

The private source may determine why a visual matters. This repository should receive only what has been deliberately approved for publication:

```text
private visual/canon work
        |
        v
public-release decision
        |
        v
public-safe description + approved asset
        |
        v
provenance / spoiler / metadata review
        |
        v
manifest-backed publication
```

Never copy private prompts, source paths, issue references, rejected candidates, hidden symbolism, scene-level rationale, or private revision identifiers into this repository.

## Review checklist

- [ ] The asset clearly belongs to one of the four book identities or to the shared series identity.
- [ ] Cross-book visual similarity is public-safe without private explanation.
- [ ] Character appearance is approved rather than exploratory.
- [ ] The image does not accidentally expose hidden plot or symbolic architecture.
- [ ] Severe material is not decontextualized into spectacle.
- [ ] Rights, provenance, metadata, spoiler tier, and approval state are known.
- [ ] Alt text describes visible content rather than private meaning.
- [ ] Deployable assets and derivatives are manifest-backed.
